# The rubric

The six registers a moment, beat, or scene can serve, and the test for how loudly it lands. Used to score moments for the trove, to evaluate every beat and scene, and to fill the register bullets of an event. Entry format is described in `../README.md`.

---

### The rubric

**What.** Six registers: surprise, comedy, tragedy, creepy, character, cool. One rubric, used both to score what a passage does and to mark how loudly it does it.

**When.** Scoring any moment for the trove; assessing any beat or scene, in planning or in prose; filling a chapter's or event's tracking bullets; deciding whether a proposed change makes a passage stronger.

**How.** Each register has a subtle form and an unsubtle form. A passage is unsubtle in a register when it produces the reader reaction in the third column.

| Register | What it does | Unsubtle when |
|---|---|---|
| **Surprise** | The story turns; a stake lands; something the reader did not see coming arrives | A gasp of shock, dismay, or exultation |
| **Comedy** | The reader is amused | Out-loud laughter |
| **Tragedy** | Grief: the loss of something the story made the reader love, paid in the currency it was given in | The reader has to stop and dab at their tears |
| **Creepy** | Dread; the wrong thing in the familiar place; the uncanny under the ordinary | A chill; the reader looks up from the page |
| **Character** | Who someone is, shown; a change in them, or in how the reader sees them | A gasp-and-pause of realization |
| **Cool** | Vivid, iconic imagery or action that imprints itself | "Damn, how cool was that" (Éowyn's "I am no man"; Daenerys walking out of the fire) |

**Scoring.** A passage is scored by how many registers it serves. The best serves all six; one that serves none is disqualified; between, more is stronger. Halves are allowed. Record the score as `registers: surprise, character, + half tragedy (2½)`, and mark the passage `subtle` or `unsubtle`, naming the register it is unsubtle in. Scoring follows the gate, never precedes it: a passage that cannot be justified in mechanical or motivational terms is out regardless of score (see *Motivation first* in `../method/outline-and-beats.md`).

**Uses beyond the trove.** A beat's notes can carry its rubric line so the derivation knows what the beat is for. A chapter can be read back against the rubric to see which registers it never touched. A pool or a book can be calibrated by the proportion of unsubtle passages (see *Calibration* in `../method/moment-trove.md`). A rework is targeted by naming the register in which the passage is weak.

**Concordance** from the vocabularies this rubric replaces: Kestrel's five *functions* (drama, comedy, tragedy, character, cool) and five unsubtle kinds (gasp, laugh, tears, realization, cool), where *drama* and *gasp* are now *surprise*; Reptile House's *Funny* and *Creepy* tracking bullets, which are the comedy and creepy registers. Entries in the source projects that use the old tags read accordingly.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4's five functions and five unsubtle kinds (September 7–8 2026), merged with Reptile House's *Creepy* tracking bullet, with *gasp* renamed *surprise*. Merged and generalized September 10 2026 (the author's).

**Scope.** Fiction.
