# Character and comedy

Instruments for casting, motivating, and voicing an ensemble. Entry format is described in `../README.md`.

---

### The character catechism

**What.** For every character in a beat: what they want, what they know, what they do, and why they would be in the room.

**When.** Every derivation; every cast ruling.

**How.** Cast by motive first; a character who has no reason to be in the room is not in it, whatever the plot would like. The catechism lives in the notes file per beat, and a *what each person wants* roster (one line per character, per movement) lives at the top of the moment trove. A character's catechism at book scale (the wants that run the whole book) lives in the constitution's character geometry and is ratified there.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, August 2026 (a method ruling). Reptile House's per-event *Motivation* bullet is the same instrument at event granularity.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Comedy serves character

**What.** Every joke reveals something about who is telling it and where they are on the book's central axis.

**When.** Any book with more than one comic voice.

**How.** Build the **comedy spectrum**: a table mapping each character's position on the book's transformation axis to a humor style and the function that style serves. Reptile House's, as the example of the shape:

| Position on the axis | Humor style | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Uninitiated human | Transgressive mockery | Insists on reality; punctures pretension |
| Resisting | Dry observational wit | Creates distance; names absurdity to separate from it |
| At a threshold | Buried references, sound-alikes | Exists between noticing and not-noticing |
| At a threshold (another) | Irony, bathos | The gap between intention and outcome |
| Mostly changed | Wordplay, puns | Liminal by nature; lives between meanings |
| Long unchanged | Garden-path jokes, non sequiturs | Meaning that turns wrong; destabilizing |
| Fully changed | Structure without soul | The pattern of humor without the person |

The kind of jokes a character makes tells the reader how far along they are, which means humor can carry a transformation the prose never states. Interaction dynamics (which pairs produce which kind of exchange) and deployment notes (where each voice is first established; "one speech maximum; let the horror land once, hard") follow the table.

**Origin.** Reptile House, `CHARACTER_VOICE_COMEDY_GUIDE_v5.md`, December 2025.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### The active protagonist

**What.** The protagonist drives events through motivated choices. She does not receive exposition passively.

**When.** Always.

**How.** Every event is driven by her motivation or by dominoes knocked over by earlier motivated actions. She investigates, pulls threads, builds spreadsheets. Her arc ends with agency, in a line she chooses with full knowledge. The test for any event: what did she do to cause it, or what did she do earlier that caused the thing that caused it?

**Origin.** Reptile House `CLAUDE.md`, principle 4, March 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### The ensemble inventory

**What.** A compact reference listing every member of a large ensemble with the same fields per member, so that operations, cast rulings, and availability can be checked at a glance.

**When.** Any book with a large recurring ensemble.

**How.** Reptile House's fields: mundane aspect (who they are as a person), archetypal aspect (what they are becoming), attributes (what they can do), tactical role, availability. Kestrel's crew file uses kind, role, and heart canon (their love life and their loss). Pick fields that answer the questions derivations actually ask. A companion **tactical framework** (how the ensemble's capabilities combine; preferred operational shape; what the opposition can bring) governs how any set piece gets planned.

**Origin.** Reptile House, `8.LEAGUE_ENSEMBLE_INVENTORY.md` and `9.TACTICAL_FRAMEWORK.md`, 2026; Kestrel, `crew.md`, August 2026.

**Scope.** Pattern (the instance is project-specific; the shape transfers).

### The consequence network as story engine

**What.** The protagonists' method of doing good generates dependents, and every dependent is a story hook.

**When.** Any ensemble whose activity leaves people behind who owe them, depend on them, or are exposed by them.

**How.** State the rule of the network ("every extraction creates a dependent; every dependent a story hook") and the constraint that makes it fragile (fiscal solvency; paper trails; the fact that the network is visible in exactly the medium the heroes cannot fight). The network then generates stories from the heroes' own past successes, and the best of those stories attack the network through its constraint rather than through the heroes.

**Origin.** Reptile House, Event 19 worldbuilding, March 2026 (the associate network; the Financial Thread candidate that grew from it).

**Scope.** Pattern.

### The ordinary-in-own-eyes protagonist

**What.** The POV character remains ordinary in their own estimation even after they are revealed to be extraordinary, and the ordinariness is true, so the gaze confirms it rather than being fooled by it.

**When.** Descent stories; any protagonist whose gift must not become a superpower.

**How.** See *The faculty that cannot be aimed* in `magic-and-metaphysics.md` for the four reasons that stack. On the page: the voice never comments on itself; the reader learns from how others look at the character and from the pattern of what the hunches turn up; the character reports the pattern and draws no conclusion about the instrument. The gap is where the character lives. Precedent: Gawain and the green girdle. The court sees a hero; Gawain sees the flinch; both readings are accurate and his is the one he is entitled to.

**Origin.** Reptile House, `TOLLIVER_SERIES_DESIGN.md`, September 8 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Choosing the investigator by who owns the case

**What.** The three investigator types (counterintelligence agent, police detective, private eye) differ in who owns the case, and that decides what kind of story they can carry.

**When.** Choosing a protagonist for an investigation into the strange.

**How.** The detective's case belongs to the state and can be taken. The agent's case belongs to an institution that already knows, so being read in is always available and being told breaks. The private eye's case belongs to a client, a person who has lost something, so the case is a bargain. Nobody can read him in, nobody takes the file, nobody is coming. He has no jurisdiction, so he can go anywhere, and no institution, so he can never be told. He discovers everything or stops. The ending available to each is different: the agent's last question is whether he accepts the briefing; the PI's is whether he keeps the case.

**Origin.** Reptile House, `TOLLIVER_SERIES_DESIGN.md`, September 8 2026.

**Scope.** Genre (investigation).

### The secret-keeper's evasion is grief, not strategy

**What.** A character keeping a structural secret from the protagonist is not the villain. Every steer, every wrong memory, every watchful look is fear of a specific thing happening again.

**When.** Any book whose emotional reveal is that the closest person was hiding something.

**How.** Give the evasion a specific terror with a specific mechanism the reader can later read in a document ("subject isolated; no outside contact"). The protagonist rereads the whole catalog of the friend's behavior with the mechanism in hand, and every item converts from control to love. The denouement's commitment ("I'm staying") then means staying because the deception had a shape, not despite it.

**Origin.** Reptile House `CLAUDE.md` pitfalls and `10.BLAIR_DETONATION_ARCHITECTURE.md`, March 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Interior contact never owns a choice

**What.** When the story stages power reaching inside persons (knowledge written into a brain, hands moved by operators, near-perfect models of minds), it never transfers authorship of a choice.

**When.** Any setting with mind-reading, possession, uplift, or perfect prediction.

**How.** State the invariant in the constitution and keep every instance consistent with it. A related invariant: a person's outer picture (species, label, legal status) is never the person; the book's thesis error is mistaking the picture for the person, and the opening stages that error in miniature.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, constitution, August 2026 (a thematic invariant of that book; recorded here as the example of a constitution stating one).

**Scope.** Pattern (the invariant is the book's; the practice of stating thematic invariants as constraints is general).
