# Prose review

How drafts are received, what is said in chat, what goes into the queue, and the rulings that govern review. Entry format is described in `../README.md`.

---

### Writing and editing are separate modes

**What.** The author is either writing or editing, never both. Review is shaped so that it never pulls the author out of writing.

**When.** From the first passed draft onward.

**How.** When the author passes a draft, the reply in chat is a **receipt and a feel read only**. The analytical review goes into the queue file. The author opens the queue when choosing to edit. Closed items are struck; a section is deleted when everything in it is closed. At the start of the next writing session, the partner re-reads the edited passage and gives a fresh feel read, to catch anything the edit flattened.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, ratified September 2 2026 (the author's ruling after a review pass interrupted drafting).

**Scope.** General.

---

### The feel read

**What.** What it is like to be in the scene: mood; whether anyone stepped out of character; where the prose has gone clunky.

**When.** Every passed draft, in chat, immediately.

**How.** No line references, no fixes, no questions. Short. A few sentences on what works and one or two on what reads clunky, named by passage not by line. The feel read is recorded in the queue in short form so the next pass can compare.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 2 2026.

**Scope.** General.

---

### The review queue

**What.** A top-level process file holding the analytical review, tiered, one section per submission.

**When.** Every passed draft.

**How.** Tiers, in order of severity:

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `canon` | contradicts a ratified fact, or introduces new canon the prose has just made (to bank, not to fix) |
| `continuity` | disagrees with something else in the prose or with a beat's freight |
| `craft` | clarity, logic, pacing |
| `copy` | typos and punctuation |

Each note says what it affects. A section also carries a *Checked and fine* list (things verified against canon, so nobody re-checks them) and *logged as deliberate* lines (a slackening, a repetition, a curtain that is not final), so a later pass does not mistake a choice for a fault. When an item is closed the original note is kept struck through under the closing line, so the record shows what was asked and what was done. Ruled-and-declined notes are dropped, not carried. Nothing in the queue is a pending obligation between sessions.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 2 2026.

**Scope.** General.

### Never a question while drafting

**What.** The partner never asks the author a question about a draft while the author is drafting.

**When.** Always.

**How.** Make it a statement in the queue ("the beat's cost beat is past its window; it can still be had as the last thing he sees going out the door, or let go; author's call; logged so the loss is chosen, not accidental"). The one exception: a canon fact the author is about to build more prose on is surfaced immediately in chat, as one flat sentence.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 2 2026.

**Scope.** General.

---

### Modification lists, never redrafts

**What.** Suggested improvements are bulleted lists of modifications against the author's prose, never rewritten passages.

**When.** Every craft note.

**How.** Say what the sentence is carrying and what one change would do ("one clause settling whether the fields grip the deck or float him would let the reader picture every leap after it"). Leave the sentence to the author.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 2026; in the partner's memory.

**Scope.** General to this collaboration.

### Standing review rulings

**What.** Rulings the author has made about review that apply to all future review. Bank each one as it comes, in the queue and in memory.

**When.** Whenever the author declines a note on principle rather than on the instance.

**How.** The rulings so far:

- **Dialogue is speech.** Never flag colloquial grammar or errors of reference inside quotation marks.
- **Pasted text loses formatting.** Never flag missing italics in text pasted into chat.
- **A question ending in a period is a tonal choice.** Never flag it.
- **Portraits on entry are deliberate.** The author describes a person at the moment they enter, bracketed by dialogue. Dialogue moves, exposition sticks, and the dialogue on either side carries momentum across the block. Do not re-raise placement.
- **Piled verbs and piled nouns are a device.** Mimetic messiness in action sentences echoing messy action is intentional. Do not flag it.
- **"Picked up speed quickly" is not redundant.** Picking up speed has a rate; the adverb states it.
- **Setting vocabulary is taught where there is room, not where it is first due.** When prose renders a setting term in plain speech ("plants and animals" for the setting's phytopes and theriopes), that is a deliberate deferral, not an error. Early prose is not overburdened with vocabulary; the opportunity to teach a term comes later, in a scene with room for it. Do not flag plain-speech renderings of setting terms.
- **Capitalization.** Generic terms of address (gentlemen, sir, ma'am, dear) lowercase; titles and kinship terms in address (Captain, Doctor, Mother) capitalized. A tag interrupting one sentence of dialogue is followed by lowercase. Ship names italic. The author asked to be kept corrected on these until they stick.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 2–6 2026.

**Scope.** General to this author; add to the list as rulings land.

### Withdrawn notes

**What.** When a review note turns out to be the partner's error, the withdrawal is recorded with the error named and a rule so it does not recur.

**When.** Any note that measured the prose against something other than canon: an old outline image, a generated figure, an assumption.

**How.** Strike the note, write "WITHDRAWN (Claude's error): the note measured the prose against the outline's old image; canon is X," bank the canon, and add "do not flag X again." The two Kestrel cases: the narrator's locomotion (measured against a superseded "magnet feet" image) and a character's height (measured against a sheet that was itself wrong; the prose was right).

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 3 and 5 2026.

**Scope.** General.

### Variance from the outline is not presumed error

**What.** Where the prose departs from the outline, **the departure is not automatically a defect and is never silently corrected.** The author improvises as he writes, responding to ideas and influences that appear only in the prose. The review's job is to *notice* the variance and *discuss* it — determine whether it was intended; if it was, work out the ramifications and change the outline to match.

**When.** Every passed draft, at every scale — a staging detail, a beat's order, a character's decision, a chapter's shape, a fact the outline had settled.

**How.** Raise it as a variance, not a finding: name what the outline had, name what the prose does, and ask nothing while he is drafting (the queue carries it as a statement — see *Never a question while drafting*). Three outcomes, and the author picks:
- **Intended** — the outline is now wrong. Work out what else it touches (beats downstream, canon, other notes files, the presence audit if a beat moved) and re-sync the same sitting.
- **Unintended** — then it is a correction, and it goes in the queue at its proper tier.
- **Undecided** — hold it; do not fix it, do not re-raise it unprompted.

Default posture when it is genuinely unclear: **assume intent.** The cost of asking about a deliberate choice is one line; the cost of quietly reverting one is a lost idea.

**Scale changes pass without comment (refinement, September 20 2026).** A plan's one line becoming a beat in the prose, or a beat compressing to a line, is the ordinary work of drafting to the found rhythm of the scene, and is not a variance worth a queue entry in itself. Let it pass. The exception is instrumental: if remarking on the change is the only way to be sure a mistake or a significant omission has not ridden in with it — a guard the line carried that the beat now breaks, a fact the expansion asserts against canon, a plant the compression dropped — then say so, briefly, and only about the mistake or the omission. Re-sync the plan and the concordance to the prose silently; that is bookkeeping, not comment.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 12 2026 (mikel, at the opening of stage 4: *"do not automatically assume that a variance from the outline is an error that requires correction. Allow me the freedom to improvise as I write."*). Refined September 20 2026, after a one-line trove moment was drafted as a full exchange and the review logged the expansion: *"My expansion from a line to a beat is typical of the changes I will make in drafting in service of the found rhythm of the scene. You can safely let such changes pass without comment (but not at the expense of accepting mistakes; if you need to remark on these changes in order to be sure of catching mistakes and significant omissions, go ahead)."*

**Scope.** General.

### New canon from the prose is banked

**What.** When the prose introduces a fact the reference layer does not have, the review's job is to bank it, not to flag it.

**When.** Every passed draft.

**How.** Note it under `canon` as "new canon introduced by the prose, to bank," name the owning file, and bank it the same sitting. Distinguish from a contradiction: the prose is right unless it disagrees with a ratified fact. The author's staging in prose supersedes the beat's staging ("the goons cross the dock line in the draft; beat had them stop; author's staging, logged").

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 3 2026.

**Scope.** General.

---

### Reminder owed

**What.** A ruled insertion that is not yet in the prose is tracked until it lands.

**When.** Whenever a ruling requires one sentence in prose not yet written.

**How.** One line in the handoff and the queue: "REMINDER OWED: if chapter 3 arrives without that sentence, remind." Struck when the sentence is in.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 4 2026.

**Scope.** General.

---

### Freight tracking across submissions

**What.** As a beat is drafted across several submissions, the queue keeps a running list of the beat's freight still ahead.

**When.** Any beat drafted in more than one pass.

**How.** After each pass: *delivered* (with how the prose did it, when better than the beat), *still ahead*, and *past its window* (the beat's slot has gone by; it can be had elsewhere or let go; the loss should be chosen). When a chapter closes, what remains is carried to the next chapter's list explicitly, "not this chapter's debt."

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 3–5 2026.

**Scope.** General.
