# The moment trove

A pool of memorable moments held in reserve and consulted at the start of every chapter. Moments are scored by the rubric in `../craft/rubric.md`. Entry format is described in `../README.md`.

---

### The trove

**What.** A pool of memorable moments, in any register of the rubric, derived by walking the beats and the cast's motivations, discussed, culled, blessed, then held in reserve and consulted at the start of every chapter to see which the chapter can use.

**When.** Once the beats and the character geometry exist; before and during drafting.

**How.** A beat is what happens; a moment is what makes it land. Nothing in the trove is a beat. A moment is placed only when the prose offers an opening, never scheduled. The trove file opens with a *what each person wants* roster for the current movement (one line per character), because moments are derived from wants. Process: walk the beats → propose → discuss and cull → let the review suggest additions → iterate a couple of times → bless the pool. Consult at each chapter's start. Keep a list of moments already banked elsewhere (in beats, in canon) so they are not re-proposed.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 7 2026. The practice is the author's, from running tabletop games.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Plain language

**What.** Every entry is readable on its own: who is present, where, what happens, why it is memorable.

**When.** Every entry.

**How.** No project shorthand, no beat keys as the only locator, no coined labels. If a reader of the novel could not follow the description, rewrite it. The first pass of the Kestrel trove was rewritten the same day for this reason.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 7 2026.

**Scope.** General.

---

### Calibration

**What.** A quarter to a third of the pool must be unsubtle.

**When.** Assessing the pool as a whole.

**How.** Unsubtle is defined per register by the rubric (`../craft/rubric.md`). Subtle moments are good; the quota exists because a pool derived by discussion drifts subtle. A second pass dedicated to unsubtle moments is the way to fill it.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 7 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

---

### Qualification and ranking

**What.** Two tests, applied in order, recorded on every discussed entry.

**When.** Every entry that reaches discussion.

**How.** **The gate:** a moment that cannot be justified in mechanical or motivational terms (the mechanism does not work; nobody present would do that; the world forbids it) is disqualified unless it can be reconstructed to fix the defect. **The ranking:** the rubric score (`../craft/rubric.md`). Record both: `gate: passes · registers: surprise, character (2)`. Also record the POV verdict where the book has a fixed narrator (see the presence audit in `outline-and-beats.md`).

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 8 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

---

### Verdicts

**What.** The status vocabulary for entries.

**When.** Every entry.

**How.** `candidate` (proposed, not discussed) → `blessed` (ratified into the pool) / `declined` (culled, with reason, never re-raised) / `regroove` (sent back: the idea is sound but the moment as written is weak; it returns only reworked into something stronger in at least one register of the rubric (`../craft/rubric.md`); the entry records what is weak so the rework has a target). `spent` when used, with the chapter. A blessed entry that is later folded into another entry says so and points at it; a superseded version is kept in one line so it is not re-proposed. Declined entries keep their full text so the reason is legible later.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 8–9 2026. "Regroove" is from the Firesign Theatre.

**Scope.** Fiction.

---

### Placement on blessing

**What.** When a moment is blessed, place it at once only if a really good placement is already obvious; otherwise hold it unplaced.

**When.** At blessing.

**How.** A placed entry names the beat. When a moment's gate depends on staging that no beat has fixed yet, fix the staging now — as a rostered beat, out of story order if need be — rather than defer it to the beat pass: deferring loses the dependency analysis the ruling just built and forces it to be rebuilt later (Kestrel, September 11 2026: the crew's first sight of the antagonist was staged as a beat six events past the frontier because a trove entry needed it). An unplaced entry records only its constraints: who must be present, earliest point, register, and any budget it counts against ("counts against the budget of small early moments"). No candidate lists of possible placements. Recurring moments carry a cap ("three uses in the whole book, no more, or it becomes a tic").

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 8 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

---

### Regroove discipline

**What.** The regroove bin is not worked on its own. It is watched against the beat list.

**When.** Whenever a beat is in development.

**How.** Check the bin for a moment that could be fitted to that beat, and redesign or restructure the moment to fit the chosen beat. That is legitimate. A regrooved moment comes back for a ruling as a new proposal against a named beat. A moment can also be gated on an undecided canon question; those are listed separately and wait for the ruling.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 10 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

---

### Authors' knowledge and plants

**What.** A blessed moment may carry, under it, what the authors know that the reader does not, and what later payoffs it makes available.

**When.** Any moment that is a plant.

**How.** A sub-entry headed *Authors' knowledge (the plant)* states the true mechanism behind the moment, who in the world knows it, and the payoffs available later (each one optional). It is not a commitment to any payoff; it is the record that makes a later payoff consistent.

**Origin.** Kestrel Book 4, September 8 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.
