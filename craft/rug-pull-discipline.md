# The rug-pull discipline

How a story generates and manages astonishment: the mechanism, its warrants, the requirements, the palette, the experts, the assembler, the three ladders, and the instruments for checking a proposed pull and annotating a route. Entry format is described in `../README.md`.

The discipline was written for a setting where the astonishing thing is the supernatural, but nothing in it depends on that. The rug can be anything that breaks the rules a competent person runs on.

---

### The mechanism

**What.** A specific pleasure: watching a highly competent operative encounter events that completely break the rules they operate under. Not the astonishment of a fool, which proves nothing. The astonishment of the person accustomed to knowing more than anyone in the room.

**When.** Any story whose engine is the disclosure of a world stranger than its characters' models of it.

**How.** A competent operative is a compressed model of how the world works. When the operative is astonished, the world broke. The operative is the reader's instrument, and the pleasure is watching a trusted instrument peg. The reference case: an operative surveys thirteen copies of one dead man and concludes that someone has made a breakthrough in cloning. That is good reasoning, the best hypothesis available to a man who does not know about the box, and he is going to be destroyed by his own rigor, because fingerprints are exactly what a man like that checks, and clones do not share them. The astonishment is earned by his competence, not despite it. The goal is a long arc that sets this up repeatedly, with the mastery a good thriller writer brings to setting up a hero's mastery of tactics.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026 (the author's). Reference points: the operative in *Dark Matter* season two; Daenerys and the pyre; Parker's handling of Spenser; the author's Redcap-and-Navarro vignette.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Warrants: irony and wonder

**What.** A rug pull needs a warrant: something that makes the reader accept the astonishment rather than suspect a cheat. There are two, and they are different pleasures.

**When.** Designing any pull; deciding where the reader sits.

**How.** **Irony:** the reader holds the truth and enjoys the operative's confident wrong model. The pleasure is anticipation: the thrill of realizing a scene is *setting up* a gobsmacking. **Wonder:** the reader and the operative are astonished together. Nobody knew about the dragons. The pleasure is the miracle plus the trust that the story will justify it. That trust is credit: the story has shown it pays its debts, so the reader lends belief before the explanation arrives. Neither requires the reader to be ahead; for something miraculous it is arguably better if they are not.

**The conversion.** In a long arc the two turn into each other automatically. The first break is wonder, on credit. From then on the reader holds what that operative learned, so the next operative, arriving with a model that does not include it, is enjoyed as irony without extra setup. A relay of experts is a machine that turns wonder into irony one rung at a time, and each rung's wonder is fresh because it breaks a bigger model than the last.

**What wonder costs.** The operative must remain the reader's instrument through the break. If the reader does not know more than he does, his reasoning has to be the reader's reasoning: we watch him build the wrong model and watch him dismantle it with evidence he would accept. This constrains who holds the camera at the moment of astonishment.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026. The two warrants and "wonder needs no foreknowledge" are the author's; the conversion is a derivation accepted without objection.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Rank and model

**What.** Rank can be bought in a sentence. A model has to be demonstrated.

**When.** Deciding how much setup a pull needs.

**How.** Genre gives the reader a complete template for the operative who knows more than anyone, and the reader lends competence on credit: one line plus five minutes of officials deferring establishes rank. The first astonishment lands on the template. Deference is enough. But deference confers rank, not a model. Watching the operative work gives the reader a specific picture of what he believes and how he reasons, so that when it breaks, the break is specific and legible: this particular assumption, which the reader watched him rely on, just failed. Deference works once and does not reload. So the first prestige can be bought cheaply; every prestige after that, on the same operative, has to be set up by demonstration, because after each adaptation he holds a new model and the reader has to watch him build it in order to watch it fail. This is not competence display for its own sake. It is exposure of the current model, done as competence so that it is fun.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026 (the author's pushback).

**Scope.** Fiction.

### The five requirements

**What.** What any rug pull must satisfy.

**When.** Every pull.

**How.**

1. **Never make the operative stupid.** The wrong model must be the best model available, one the reader would have built too without privileged knowledge.
2. **Legible in retrospect.** The break must be airtight by the operative's own standards. Spectacle does not astonish a professional. Evidence does.
3. **Paid in the operative's own currency.** The instrument that breaks him is the one he trusts most. The fingerprints.
4. **Everything learned stays true.** A pull that revokes an earlier fact is a cheat. One that reframes it is a rung. (The anti-*Twin Peaks* rule.)
5. **Every pull yields a puzzle piece.** Each one supplies some small piece whose solution gets the story up the next rung of the ladder. The cleanest guarantee: the piece is produced by the operative's own procedure. **The expert is a sensor and the rug pull is the reading.** That is what makes the pieces trustworthy: they were gathered by the best available method.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026. Requirements 1–4 and the puzzle-piece requirement are the author's; expert-as-sensor is an accepted derivation.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Judiciousness

**What.** The rules that keep the mechanism alive across a long story.

**When.** Planning the sequence of pulls in a story and across a series.

**How.**

- **Not every chapter.**
- **Never the same rug pull twice in one story.**
- **No predictable rhythm.** Intervals must vary, and not simply shorten as the story escalates.
- **Setup and nature must vary** enough across scenes and stories to stay unexpected. The palette exists so that no combination is reused.
- **Some experts must be right and stay right.** If every expert who walks on gets pulled, the reader learns the rule in three chapters and the mechanism dies. Expertise must keep its credibility for pulls to cost anything. A story needs experts who are pulled, experts who hold, and at least one setup that looks like a pull and is not. The reader must not be able to predict which.
- **A long shaggy dog masks the interval.** A reader waiting for secret A thinks they know where the next pull is and stops counting.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026. The first four are the author's; experts that hold is an accepted derivation.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### The palette

**What.** Two tables of axes. The impact and flavor of a pull is never set by the pull or its setup alone; it is always the combination of those with the character, framework, stake, and expectations of the person under whom the rug is pulled.

**When.** Designing a pull; checking that a combination has not been used before.

**How.** The same impossible revelation hits an arrogant know-it-all officer, a perpetually astonished hobbyist, and an old-hand occultist very differently, and differs again depending on whether the target is closing cold cases, trying to learn what happened to her missing mother, or collecting evidence for a pet theory.

**Axes of the pull**

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| **What gets pulled** | Method (the instrument fails). Authority (the room stops deferring). Categories (nothing in his bins). Allies (his own institution knew). Self (he is the anomaly). Scale (he was right, and it is far bigger). |
| **Direction** | Expected less weirdness, got more. Expected the wrong weirdness. Expected weirdness and got a man with a shovel. |
| **Who pulls** | The evidence. A rival expert. The phenomenon itself. The expert, on reflection. |
| **Timing of payoff** | In the room, almost immediately. That night, when he checks. Chapters later, when a piece from someone else's pull lands on his desk. |
| **Setup shape** | Set up and pay off at once. Or the shaggy dog: build conviction that we are uncovering secret A, then uncover secret B, possibly hitherto entirely unknown. |
| **Reader's seat** | Ahead (irony). Beside (wonder). Behind: the expert knows something the reader does not, and his going still is how the reader learns how bad it is. |
| **Register** | Comic, horrifying, wondrous, sad. |

**Axes of the target**

| Axis | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Temperament** | Arrogant. Perpetually astonished. Old hand. Unflappable by policy. |
| **Framework** | What the target expects the world to be. Decides *what counts as astonishing*: an occultist is not astonished by a ghost. The pull must land outside the framework. The best land in the gap between two frameworks: the expert who is **nearly right**. Near-right is a better rug than ignorant, because the expert's own knowledge is what breaks. |
| **Stake** | Why the target is looking. Decides *what the pull costs*. Same revelation: the cold-case officer loses a clearance, the hobbyist gains a following, the woman looking for her mother loses her twice. Stake moves a pull between comic and tragic without changing the revelation, and keeps the reader from reading pulls as a trick. |
| **Adaptation style** | What the target does afterward. Denial (writes "undetermined"). Conversion (the hobbyist becomes a zealot). Incorporation (the occultist adds a page and carries on: the most chilling, the least dramatic). Collapse. Refusal (the toothpick). A target with a good adaptation style can be pulled again with a different rug. One who collapses is spent. |

**The perpetually astonished target** cannot be gobsmacked with more weirdness. You do it by making them *right* (everyone finally believes him, and it is no relief), or by giving them the mundane.

**Tendencies, not rules.** Speed suits the arrogant target (comedy wants it fast). Duration suits the grieving one. The exceptions are where the real surprises live.

**Disciplines for the shaggy dog.** The evidence for A must be honest: a rereader must see that a reasonable person would have built A. B must explain the A-evidence better, not replace it. B is strongest as a category shift, not a substitution: not "the killer was the other brother" but "there was no killer; there were two of him."

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026. The target axes of character, framework, and stake, and immediate-versus-shaggy-dog setup, are the author's; the tables, adaptation style, and the shaggy-dog disciplines are accepted derivations.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Experts, genuine and self-appointed

**What.** A story supplies a sequence of experts, genuine or self-appointed or both, to pull the rug from under by different mechanisms.

**When.** Casting a story built on this discipline.

**How.** Genuine experts supply irony and wonder. Self-appointed experts (the podcaster, the debunker, the psychic, the man with a chart) supply comedy and, better, misdirection: sometimes the crank is right, which pulls the rug from under the reader rather than a character. Build the roster from an **expert ecology**: the communities in the setting that hold incompatible frameworks about the anomalous, each a different kind of rug to pull, whose disagreement is the story's contested scholarship (see `magic-and-metaphysics.md`). Then build a per-setting table of **what breaks each competence**:

| Competence | What breaks it (Reptile House example) |
|---|---|
| Identity and verification (tradecraft) | Same fingerprints, two people. |
| Tactical geometry (commando) | Doors where the diagram has walls. |
| Evidence and records (investigator) | Records that settle. Witnesses whose errors agree. |
| Having the file (institution) | A player who does not participate in the framework. |
| Protecting the client (bodyguard) | A thing that cannot be shot. |
| Perception itself (sketch artist, surveillance tech) | The mind slides off. |
| Physical law (the scientist) | The precision of futility. |

The table is the setting's inventory of rugs. Each row is a pull not yet spent. The "behind" seat belongs to the character who has been inside the strangeness longest: their astonishment tells the reader more than any explanation could.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026 (the sequence of experts is the author's; the table is derived). The expert ecology came from an Event 23 session earlier in 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction; the table is per setting.

### The assembler

**What.** If every pull yields a puzzle piece and the pieces climb a ladder, someone is putting them together. If it is the reader alone, the book is a puzzle. If it is a character, that character is by definition not one of the experts being pulled, at least at first.

**When.** Choosing the protagonist's relation to the pulls.

**How.** Two flavors, two reader seats:

- **Runs toward the pull.** Wants the rug gone. Collects what experts drop; gets louder as the weirdness climbs. The reader sits beside her, and her delight is the reader's permission to enjoy it. She is a self-appointed expert who graduates into a genuine one. Her relation to experts: she interviews them.
- **Declines the pull.** Refuses to be gobsmacked; gets quieter as the weirdness climbs, and files it. This puts the reader in a seat the first flavor cannot offer: ahead of the character *about his own astonishment*. Comedy and dread both live in that gap. His relation to experts: he audits them. A lifetime of finding where the expert was wrong makes him a rug-puller by trade.

The flavors set the reader's seat, so they are not interchangeable within a story without deliberately moving the reader between beside and ahead. A third protagonist can combine them (runs toward the pull, files it like the second).

**The assembler graduates.** An assembler who climbs becomes an expert, and an expert can be pulled. The person who solved rung three is the natural victim of rung four. This is the relay built into one character, and it is the ladder's last rung (the investigator is not separate from what they investigate) arriving as plot mechanism rather than theme.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026 (accepted derivation; the two flavors are the two protagonists of the setting).

**Scope.** Fiction.

### The three ladders

**What.** Three distinct objects that must be kept distinct: the setting's cosmological ladder, each story's disclosure ladder, and the reader's genre expectation.

**When.** Designing a story within a series; checking that a story's shape differs from its predecessors'.

**How.**

**The mountain** is the setting's ladder of destinations, fixed (see `magic-and-metaphysics.md`).

**The route** is each story's disclosure ladder: the sequence of pulls that gradually discloses that story's level of weirdness and the full shape of its stakes and arc. The first story starts by disclosing completely ordinary weirdness (the mundane case turns out to involve organized crime, a conspiracy, smugglers worse than expected), then, early, the first weirdness pull: something much stranger is going on. Story two builds its ladder around disclosing a *differently shaped* stakes and arc. Different routes show different faces of the mountain; the reader who knows the mountain still does not know the route. **Some routes should go down.** Disciplines:

- **The genre pull happens inside the earlier genre's own logic.** Every procedure of the crime story stays valid; what changes is the ontology underneath. The reader keeps the pleasure they signed up for and gets the other thing too.
- **Ordinary first**, for two reasons: it calibrates trust (the story pays its first debts in mundane currency before asking credit for a miracle), and it sets the shaggy dog (the reader recalibrates once, "worse than I thought," concludes recalibration is over, and is standing on the rug when it goes). "Worse, then other" is a move to use once in a series.
- **Stagger the first weird pull onto a secondary target.** The reader trusts a criminal's judgment about whether a man shot in the head is dead; the protagonist's model stays intact for a bigger rug later; the reader gets irony in the meantime.
- **Stakes and weirdness climb together but not in lockstep.** The tight version: one event raises both. The loose version, for variety: a pull that raises weirdness while lowering stakes (very strange, and it brought tea). A valence flip breaks rhythm better than any change of interval.

**The reader's genre expectation** is the strongest framework a reader carries, so the genre pull is the largest rug available and the one to spend most carefully: once per story, perhaps twice, never at the same height twice across the series. Series readers cannot be pulled by level (they already know the strange thing exists). What can be pulled is the *shape*: they expect escalation and get descent; expect the heroes and get something the heroes are afraid of; expect horror and get a heist.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026. The disclosure ladder and "differently shaped stakes and arc" are the author's; the three-ladder distinction, staggering, lockstep, and the shape pull are accepted derivations.

**Scope.** Fiction, series.

### Checklist for a proposed rug pull

**What.** Twelve questions to ask of any proposed pull.

**When.** Before a pull goes into a synopsis or a beat.

**How.**

1. Who is the target, and what are their temperament, framework, stake, and adaptation style?
2. What exactly is pulled (method, authority, categories, allies, self, scale), and in which direction?
3. Is the target's wrong model the best available? Would the reader have built it?
4. What is the reader's seat at the moment of the break (ahead, beside, behind)?
5. What is the operative's own currency, and is the break paid in it?
6. What puzzle piece does the pull yield, and which rung does it serve?
7. Does anything previously learned get revoked? (If yes, stop.)
8. Has this combination of axes been used before in this story? In the series?
9. What interval separates it from the last pull, and is the interval predictable?
10. Which expert in this stretch of story holds, so that expertise keeps its credibility?
11. If it is a shaggy dog: is the A-evidence honest, does B explain it better, is B a category shift?
12. If it is the story's genre pull: does it happen inside the earlier genre's own logic, and is it at a different height than the last story's?

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026.

**Scope.** Fiction.

### Route annotation

**What.** The examination instrument for a story built on this discipline: a table of its pulls with palette coordinates, plus a fixed set of summary lines.

**When.** After a synopsis is written, before it is judged; also as a diagnostic on an existing book.

**How.** The table, one row per pull, in story order:

| # | Pull | Target | What's pulled / direction | Who pulls | Setup / timing | Reader seat | Register | Piece yielded | Rung |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A non-pull (a setup that looks like a pull and holds) gets a row too, with "(a pull that isn't: the method holds)" in the pulled column. The rung column may say "(mundane)", "(floor)", "(genre pull)", "(descent)", or a rung number, with "glimpse" for a rung seen and not reached.

After the table:

- **Genre pull:** which row, at roughly what fraction of the story, and inside whose logic.
- **Assembler:** who, and which flavor.
- **Experts who hold:** by name, with what holds.
- **Perimeter cost:** who pays at the edge of the story (the death nobody on either side controlled).
- **Stakes shape:** ascending, widening, narrowing, and where it changes.
- **Series-reader note:** which pulls play as irony for a series reader and as wonder for a new one; which are shape pulls.

Then the examination questions across routes: are the routes different enough (POV, direction, stakes shape, genre-to-genre, the reader's seat); does each pull pay in the target's own currency (name the weak spot); rhythm (list the intervals; neither periodic nor monotonic; where the shaggy dog sits); do the experts who hold, hold credibly; is the once-per-series move used more than once; do the routes share a spine no protagonist sees whole.

Applied to an existing book as a diagnostic, the annotation also lists divergences (what the book would do differently if built from the discipline) and finds which rung it actually climbs to.

**Origin.** Reptile House, September 9 2026 (`experiments/ROUTES.md`, `ROUTE_D_47_DETAILS.md`).

**Scope.** Fiction.
